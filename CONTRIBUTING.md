<h1>Introduction</h1>

Thank you for considering contributing to the `chromaquant` project! We're super excited to have grabbed your attention. This project is associated with the Rorrer Lab in the Department of Chemical Engineering at UW. You can find more information about our group at https://www.rorrerlab.com!

<h2>Why read these guidelines?</h2>

> We are huge supporters of collaboration, especially when it comes to academic research. Effective collaboration depends on predefined expectations and rules, which ensure the success of an open source project like this one.

<h2>What contributions are we looking for?</h2>

> ChromaQuant is an open source project for a reason – we want people to both use this project and contribute back to it for an overall improved experience. We are primarily looking for contributions in the form of bug reports, feature recommendations, and documentation. For users that are looking to contribute more significantly, we ask that they reach out to lead developer Julia Hancock at jnhanc@uw.edu prior to initiating pull requests. We are really excited to foster new collaborative connections and grow our list of contributors.

<h1>Expectations</h1>

>We expect all contributors to adhere to these expectations as well as those outlined in the Code of Conduct:

> 1. Connect with Julia directly before contributing so we can collaborate more effectively. Simple comments or bug reports don't need to follow this rule, although we may reach out for more information!
> 2. If you make tutorials or other contributions on the web outside of this repository, please let us know through an Issue or email so we can link your work to the repo.
> 3. Be welcoming to newcomers and encourage new contributors from all backgrounds and areas of research – further expectations along this line can be found in the Code of Conduct.

<h1>Conclusions</h1>

We are very excited to watch this project grow and are similarly excited to get a chance to work with collaborators across different fields and areas of expertise. In advance of a formal publication, we are seeking more engagement and collaboration to demonstrate the research impact of this project. Documentation, bug fixes, and feature requests are encouraged. Please reach out to Julia Hancock at jnhanc@uw.edu before initiating feature-heavy pull requests. Thank you!
